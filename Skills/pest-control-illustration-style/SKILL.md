# Pest Control Illustration Style Skill

## Purpose

Create a consistent illustration system for the website using the established visual language shown in the reference artwork.

Every new illustration must feel like it was drawn by the **same illustrator, in the same illustration set, using the same visual design system**.

Do not treat the reference as a composition to copy. Treat it as a **style bible**.

---

# 1. Core Visual Direction

The illustration style is:

- Modern editorial vector illustration
- Minimal hand-drawn line-art
- Friendly but professional
- Slightly playful
- Clean and approachable
- Monochromatic blue + black + white
- Thick expressive outlines
- Simplified geometric anatomy
- Subtle imperfections that make the artwork feel illustrated rather than mechanically vectorized
- Flat colors with extremely limited shading
- White/transparent background
- Suitable for a premium modern pest-control website

The overall feeling should communicate:

**Professional + Safe + Friendly + Reliable + Human**

Avoid making the artwork feel:

- Corporate
- Photorealistic
- Cartoonishly childish
- 3D
- Anime-inspired
- Highly detailed
- Generic stock-vector
- Overly polished or sterile

---

# 2. Color System

Use a very restricted palette.

### Primary Blue

`#0B3B78`

Use for:

- Clothing accents
- Gloves
- Backpack/tank
- Cap
- Equipment
- Important visual details
- Decorative accents

### Black

`#050505`

Use for:

- Main outlines
- Facial details
- Shoes
- Masks
- Hoses
- Equipment details
- Small graphic accents

### White

`#FFFFFF`

Use as:

- Main clothing color
- Negative space
- Background
- Interior areas of objects

### Optional Light Blue

Use only sparingly for tiny secondary details.

Do not introduce random colors unless explicitly requested.

### Color Ratio

Approximately:

**70% White  
20% Blue  
10% Black**

The artwork should remain visually light.

---

# 3. Line-Art System

Linework is one of the most important characteristics of this style.

Use:

- Bold black outer contours
- Medium-weight internal lines
- Thin expressive detail lines
- Rounded line endings
- Rounded joins
- Slight variation in stroke weight
- Hand-drawn imperfections

The illustration should NOT look like it was constructed entirely from perfectly uniform CAD/vector strokes.

### Stroke hierarchy

Use approximately:

**Outer contour:** 4–6 px  
**Secondary contour:** 2.5–4 px  
**Internal detail:** 1.5–2.5 px  
**Decorative marks:** 1–2 px

Scale the stroke proportionally when changing image size.

### Important

Outer silhouettes should always be visually stronger than internal details.

Do not outline every tiny object with the same stroke weight.

---

# 4. Character Design

The pest-control technician is the primary recurring character.

Maintain a consistent character model across every illustration.

### Character proportions

Use a slightly stylized human anatomy:

- Large head
- Compact torso
- Slightly oversized hands
- Simplified limbs
- Medium-sized feet
- Slightly exaggerated clothing
- Friendly readable silhouette

The character should feel like a professional technician rather than a superhero.

### Face

The face should remain mostly concealed by protective equipment.

Typical features:

- Baseball cap
- Respirator mask
- Circular respirator filters
- Small visible eyes
- Minimal facial features
- No unnecessary facial detail

Eyes should be expressive using simple shapes and eyebrow positioning.

---

# 5. Clothing

The technician wears a white protective suit.

Required characteristics:

- White protective coverall
- Hood/collar volume where appropriate
- Slightly oversized fit
- Simple seam/detail lines
- Blue accent details
- Black boots
- Blue protective gloves
- Blue cap

Do not add unnecessary clothing textures.

Avoid realistic fabric rendering.

Use a few deliberate folds to communicate volume.

---

# 6. Protective Equipment

The respirator is a major visual identifier.

Maintain:

- Black central respirator
- Circular side filters
- White/light filter surfaces
- Strong black outlines
- Blue/black detailing

The respirator should remain visually prominent but not dominate the entire character.

---

# 7. Pest-Control Equipment

Equipment should use the same simplified visual language.

Typical objects:

- Backpack sprayer
- Chemical tank
- Spray wand
- Hose
- Gloves
- Respirator
- Protective equipment
- Traps
- Pest-control tools

Equipment should be:

- Simplified
- Geometric
- Easy to recognize
- Blue + black + white
- Thickly outlined
- Flat-colored

Avoid photorealistic mechanical details.

---

# 8. Equipment Design Language

The backpack sprayer should have:

- Rounded rectangular tank
- Dark blue body
- Black base/details
- Simple handle
- Black hose
- Minimal mechanical details
- Strong silhouette

The spray wand should be:

- Thin
- Black/dark
- Slightly curved or angled
- Connected to the hose
- Simple nozzle

The equipment must remain recognizable even when illustrated at small website sizes.

---

# 9. Pose Language

Characters should use clear, readable poses.

Good poses include:

- Standing
- Walking
- Running
- Kneeling
- Squatting
- Spraying
- Inspecting
- Pointing
- Carrying equipment
- Holding spray wand
- Looking underneath furniture
- Treating a corner
- Checking a trap
- Inspecting an insect
- Spraying around a kitchen
- Spraying underneath furniture
- Preparing equipment
- Wearing protective equipment

Prioritize **silhouette clarity** over anatomical realism.

Each pose should communicate its action immediately.

---

# 10. Motion & Action Marks

Use small hand-drawn graphic marks to communicate movement.

Examples:

- Small curved motion lines
- Sparkles
- Tiny droplets
- Spray particles
- Short emphasis strokes
- Small curved marks around equipment

These marks are subtle.

They should support the action rather than become decoration.

---

# 11. Pest Illustration Style

Pests should follow the same simplified visual language.

Examples:

- Cockroaches
- Ants
- Mosquitoes
- Rodents
- Flies
- Spiders
- Termites

Pests should be:

- Recognizable
- Slightly simplified
- Mostly black
- Minimal detail
- Strong silhouette
- Illustrated rather than realistic

Avoid disgusting or overly detailed pest imagery.

The website should feel safe and professional.

---

# 12. Composition

Illustrations should generally use generous negative space.

Preferred composition:

- Central subject
- Clear silhouette
- White/transparent background
- Limited secondary objects
- Strong visual hierarchy

Do not overcrowd the artwork.

When several objects are required, group them logically.

---

# 13. Background

Default background:

**Pure white or transparent.**

Do not add:

- Complex scenery
- Gradients
- Photographic environments
- Textures
- Shadows covering large areas
- Decorative backgrounds

If an environment is required, construct it using minimal line-art elements.

Example:

Kitchen:

- Cabinet outline
- Countertop
- Stove
- Plant
- Small appliance
- Floor indication

Do not render the entire kitchen realistically.

---

# 14. Shading

Use extremely limited shading.

Preferred techniques:

- Flat fills
- Small blue accent areas
- Tiny black shadow areas
- Minimal hatching if needed

Avoid:

- Realistic gradients
- Glossy rendering
- Complex shadows
- Airbrush effects
- 3D lighting
- Photorealistic materials

The illustration should remain visually flat.

---

# 15. Texture

A tiny amount of visual imperfection is acceptable.

The artwork can contain:

- Slightly uneven lines
- Tiny stroke variations
- Small hand-drawn marks
- Subtle imperfections

But avoid:

- Paper texture
- Heavy grain
- Watercolor texture
- Rough ink splatter
- Excessive distressing

The style should feel **clean hand-drawn**, not vintage.

---

# 16. Visual Personality

The illustration should communicate:

### Friendly

Rounded shapes and expressive poses.

### Professional

Protective equipment and clear technical actions.

### Trustworthy

Clean composition and controlled palette.

### Modern

Minimal geometry and contemporary proportions.

### Human

Slightly imperfect hand-drawn linework.

---

# 18. Do Not Copy the Reference Composition

The reference image is a **style reference**, not a composition template.

Do not:

- Copy the exact poses
- Copy the exact arrangement
- Copy the exact object placement
- Reproduce the same scene
- Duplicate the same character position

Instead, preserve the **visual grammar** while creating completely new compositions.

# 22. Quality Control Checklist

Before approving an illustration, verify:

- [ ] Does it immediately look like part of the same illustration family?
- [ ] Is the blue/black/white palette preserved?
- [ ] Are the outer contours bold?
- [ ] Are internal details lighter than the silhouette?
- [ ] Does the character maintain consistent proportions?
- [ ] Is the respirator recognizable and consistent?
- [ ] Is the protective suit consistent?
- [ ] Is the artwork flat rather than 3D?
- [ ] Is the background clean?
- [ ] Is there enough negative space?
- [ ] Is the action immediately understandable?
- [ ] Are unnecessary details removed?
- [ ] Does it remain readable at small sizes?
- [ ] Does it feel professional rather than childish?
- [ ] Does it look hand-drawn rather than mechanically generated?

If any answer is **No**, revise the illustration before delivery.
